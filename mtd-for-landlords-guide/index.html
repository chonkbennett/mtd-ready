Making Tax Digital for landlords: what changes and when

If you let out property, Making Tax Digital (MTD) for Income Tax is likely to affect you sooner than you think. Many landlords assume it is only for businesses, or that it depends on their profit. Neither is true. This guide covers the points that matter specifically to landlords.

Does MTD apply to landlords?

Yes. MTD for Income Tax applies to sole traders and landlords. Whether you are in, and from when, depends on your qualifying income:

Start date Qualifying income more than In the tax year
6 April 2026 £50,000 2024 to 2025
6 April 2027 £30,000 2025 to 2026
6 April 2028 £20,000 2026 to 2027

Why your profit does not matter here

For landlords, this is the single most important point. Qualifying income is your property income before expenses. That means gross rents, before letting agent fees, repairs, insurance or anything else comes off.

A landlord whose costs leave only a modest profit can still be well over the threshold. If your gross rent is more than £30,000 a year in the 2025 to 2026 tax year, you start on 6 April 2027, regardless of what is left after costs.

Jointly owned property

Many landlords own property with a partner or family member. For MTD, only your share of the income counts towards your qualifying income.

GOV.UK's example: a jointly owned property brings in £50,000 a year and is owned equally. Each owner counts £25,000.

That share is then added to any other qualifying income each owner has. So two joint owners can end up with different start dates, depending on what else they earn.

Landlords with a job

If you are employed and also let out property, your wages taxed through PAYE do not count towards qualifying income. Only your property income, and any self-employment income, is added up.

The same applies to pensions. The State Pension and private pensions are excluded, so a retired landlord counts only the rent.

Landlords who are also self-employed

If you run a business as a sole trader and let property, the two are added together. GOV.UK gives an example: £25,000 of rental income plus £27,000 of self-employment income makes £52,000 of qualifying income. Neither is over the threshold alone; together they are.

Under MTD, your software treats these as separate businesses and sends totals for each in your quarterly updates. When you choose software, make sure it handles both property and self-employment.

What changes day to day

Once MTD applies, you:

  • keep digital records of rental income and expenses
  • send a quarterly update to HMRC through compatible software, by 7 August, 7 November, 7 February and 7 May
  • submit your tax return through the software after the year ends

Quarterly updates are summaries of income and expenses, not tax returns, and they are cumulative: each covers the tax year to date. If you miss an expense in an early quarter, add it to your records and the next update includes it.

For jointly owned property, GOV.UK notes you can resend your final quarterly update to add expenses you missed, and to record a Rent a Room claim.

Practical steps for landlords

  1. Total your gross rents for 2025 to 2026, using your share for joint property.
  2. Add any self-employment turnover. Leave out wages, pensions and dividends.
  3. Check the result against the table above to find your start date.
  4. Separate your rental money from personal spending if you have not already. A dedicated account makes digital records far easier.
  5. Choose software that supports property income, and self-employment too if you need it.
  6. Sign up for MTD for Income Tax and authorise your software before your start date.

Choosing software as a landlord

Landlords have some specific needs. GOV.UK advises checking that software supports all your income sources and your accounting period, and can submit your tax return as well as quarterly updates. You can choose software that creates your records, or bridging software that works with a spreadsheet you already keep. Free options exist for simple tax affairs.

If you have several properties, or property alongside a business, record-keeping software that tracks each income stream separately can save time.

Before signing up for anything, check the product appears in HMRC's software finder for MTD for Income Tax and supports property income.

Common questions from landlords

My rent is over £30,000 but my mortgage interest and repairs take most of it. Am I in? Yes, if the gross rent for 2025 to 2026 was more than £30,000. The test uses income before expenses, so costs do not bring you under the threshold.

I only have one property. Does MTD still apply? The number of properties does not matter. What matters is your qualifying income: rent from all your properties, your share of any jointly owned ones, plus any self-employment income.

My spouse and I own our rental equally. Do we both need to use MTD? Each of you looks at your own qualifying income. Your half of the rent is added to anything else you each earn from self-employment or property, so one of you may be in before the other.

I am under £30,000 this year. Can I forget about it? Not yet. The threshold drops to £20,000 from 6 April 2028, based on the 2026 to 2027 tax year. If your rents are between the two, plan for 2028.

If you use a letting agent

A letting agent collecting rent does not change your MTD obligations; the income is still yours to report. Ask your agent for statements in a format you can bring into your software regularly, rather than once a year.
