MTD Ready

Free MTD software: who it suits and what to check

Making Tax Digital (MTD) for Income Tax means many sole traders and landlords will need compatible software for the first time. Free software can work for some people, but it depends on your income and records. This guide covers who needs MTD, when free software may be enough, and what to check before you choose.

Do you need MTD software at all?

Whether you need MTD for Income Tax depends on your qualifying income. This is your total income from self-employment and property before expenses (your turnover), not your profit.

MTD for Income Tax applies in stages:

Start date Applies if your qualifying income was more than In the tax year
6 April 2026 £50,000 2024 to 2025
6 April 2027 £30,000 2025 to 2026
6 April 2028 £20,000 2026 to 2027

What counts as qualifying income

Qualifying income includes:

  • Self-employment turnover
  • Property income (rent), before expenses

It does not include:

  • Employment (PAYE) income
  • Your share of partnership profit
  • Dividends
  • The State Pension
  • Private pensions

For jointly owned property, only your share counts.

GOV.UK gives two examples:

  • Someone with £25,000 of rental income and £27,000 of self-employment income has £52,000 of qualifying income. That is over the £50,000 threshold.
  • A jointly owned property earning £50,000, owned equally, gives each owner £25,000 of qualifying income.

If your situation is less straightforward, use HMRC's eligibility checker on GOV.UK.

What MTD software has to do

If you are within MTD for Income Tax, you must:

  1. Keep digital records of your income and expenses.
  2. Use compatible software to send quarterly updates to HMRC.
  3. Sign up before your start date.

Any software you choose, free or paid, has to help you meet these requirements.

Quarterly update periods and deadlines

There are two ways to set your quarterly periods. Both use the same deadlines.

Standard period Calendar period Deadline
6 April to 5 July 1 April to 30 June 7 August
6 April to 5 October 1 April to 30 September 7 November
6 April to 5 January 1 April to 31 December 7 February
6 April to 5 April 1 April to 31 March 7 May

Each update is cumulative. It covers the start of the tax year up to the end of that period. So if you find a mistake in earlier records, you correct it in your next update. You do not need to resubmit earlier quarters.

Quarterly updates are summaries, not tax returns. For the year-end steps, check GOV.UK.

For the 2026 to 2027 tax year, HMRC will not give penalty points for late quarterly updates. This gives first-year users some room to learn the process. Aim to meet the deadlines anyway, and check GOV.UK for the rules that apply in later years.

Who free MTD software tends to suit

GOV.UK says free options exist for people with simple tax affairs. HMRC does not define a single test for "simple", but in practice free software is more likely to suit you if:

  • You have one income source, such as a single sole trader business or one rental property.
  • You have a modest number of transactions. Fewer invoices, receipts and payments means less to record and sort.
  • You already keep tidy records, for example in a spreadsheet you update regularly.
  • You do not need extra features, such as invoicing, bank feeds, payroll or detailed reports.

Free software may be less suitable if:

  • You have several income sources, such as self-employment plus property, or more than one business.
  • You own property jointly and need to record only your share.
  • You have a lot of transactions and want them imported automatically.
  • You want help with day-to-day bookkeeping, not just HMRC submissions.

None of this rules free software in or out. It simply tells you what to look at more closely.

Two types of software: which fits how you work?

GOV.UK describes two main types of compatible software.

Software that creates digital records

You record income and expenses directly in the software, which then sends your quarterly updates. This suits you if you are starting from scratch or want everything in one place.

Bridging software

Bridging software connects your existing spreadsheets to HMRC. You keep records in a spreadsheet, and the bridging software sends the figures. This suits you if your spreadsheet already works well and you do not want to move to a new system.

Some free options fall into each category. Before you decide on price, decide which type matches how you keep records.

What to check before choosing free MTD software

Use this checklist for any product, free or paid.

1. Is it on the GOV.UK software finder?

GOV.UK recommends starting with its software finder for MTD for Income Tax. Use it to filter for products that match your needs. Do not rely only on a product's own description of itself as "MTD-ready".

2. Does it support all your income sources?

Check that the software handles every source you have: self-employment, UK property, or both. A free tool that only handles one type will not be enough if you have two.

3. Does it support your accounting period?

GOV.UK specifically advises checking this. Make sure the software works with your accounting period and the quarterly periods you plan to use (standard or calendar).

4. Is it really free for your situation?

Some products are free only under certain conditions, such as a turnover limit, a number of transactions, or a limited time. Read the provider's terms. Check what happens if your income or circumstances change, and what it would cost to move to a paid plan or another provider.

5. Can you get your data out?

You need to keep records, so check whether you can export them in a usable format. This matters if you switch software later.

6. What support is available?

Find out what help is offered: guides, email, chat or phone. If you are new to MTD, support may matter more than a feature list.

7. Does it handle corrections easily?

Because updates are cumulative, you correct mistakes in later updates. Check that the software makes it simple to edit earlier records.

When paid software might be worth considering

Free software is the right choice for many people with simple affairs. Paid software may be worth considering if:

  • You have several income sources or jointly owned property
  • You have many transactions and want to spend less time entering them
  • You want invoicing, expense tracking and reporting alongside your MTD submissions
  • You work with an accountant who prefers a particular system

If you are comparing paid options, Xero is one accounting product used by UK sole traders and small businesses. As with any product, check the GOV.UK software finder to confirm it supports your income sources and accounting period before you commit.

A simple way to decide

  1. Work out your qualifying income using turnover from self-employment and property only.
  2. Find your start date from the thresholds above.
  3. List your income sources and how you keep records now.
  4. Choose a software type: digital records or bridging.
  5. Shortlist products on the GOV.UK software finder that support your income sources and accounting period.
  6. Compare free and paid options against the checklist above.
  7. Sign up for MTD for Income Tax before your start date. Check GOV.UK for how to do this.

Summary

Free MTD software can work well if your tax affairs are simple: one income source, few transactions and tidy records. Whatever you choose, it must let you keep digital records and send quarterly updates, and you must sign up before your start date. Start with the GOV.UK software finder, check the product supports your income sources and accounting period, and read the terms of any free plan. If your affairs are more complex, compare paid options too, but base your choice on what your records actually need.
