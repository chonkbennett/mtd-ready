MTD quarterly updates explained for sole traders and landlords

"Quarterly updates" is the part of Making Tax Digital (MTD) for Income Tax that worries people most. Four submissions a year sounds like four tax returns a year. It is not. This guide explains what a quarterly update actually is, when each one is due, and how corrections work.

What a quarterly update is

Every three months, your MTD-compatible software adds up your digital records for each business and sends HMRC the totals: your self-employment income and expenses, and your property income and expenses.

GOV.UK is explicit that these are summaries, not tax returns. You are not calculating a final tax bill each quarter, and you do not fill in the full set of boxes you would on a Self Assessment return. The software builds the summary from the records you have already kept.

If you have more than one business, for example a sole trader business and some let property, the software sends totals for each.

The four deadlines

There are two ways to set your update periods. Most people use the standard periods, which follow the tax year.

Standard update periods

Period covered Deadline
6 April to 5 July 7 August
6 April to 5 October 7 November
6 April to 5 January 7 February
6 April to 5 April 7 May

Calendar update periods

If your records already run on calendar quarters, you can use periods that end on 30 June, 30 September, 31 December and 31 March instead. Each starts on 1 April, and the deadlines are exactly the same: 7 August, 7 November, 7 February and 7 May.

You can send each update any time from the end of the period up to the deadline. There is no need to wait for the last day.

Why every period starts in April

Look at the tables again. Every period starts at the beginning of the tax year, not at the start of the latest three months.

That is because quarterly updates are cumulative. Each one covers everything from the start of the tax year to the end of the latest period. Your second update, for example, covers six months, not three.

This design has a practical benefit that is easy to miss.

Fixing mistakes is simpler than you think

Because each update is cumulative, you do not go back and resubmit an earlier quarter when you find a mistake. You correct the record in your software, and the next update automatically carries the corrected year-to-date totals.

Say you forgot to record a few expenses in the first quarter. When you notice, you add them to your records. Your second update includes them, because it covers the whole year to date.

GOV.UK also explains that you can resend your fourth update to correct errors, add expenses for jointly owned property that you missed, or record a Rent a Room claim.

What you need to have in place

For quarterly updates to be painless, three things need to be true before the period ends:

  1. Your records are digital and up to date. The software can only total what has been entered. Little and often beats a scramble before each deadline.
  2. Your software is authorised. You sign up for MTD for Income Tax and connect your software to HMRC before your start date.
  3. Each income source is set up correctly. If you have self-employment and property income, both need to be in the software as separate businesses.

A routine that works

Many people find a simple monthly habit is enough:

  • Weekly or monthly: record income and expenses as they happen, or check transactions your software has pulled from your bank.
  • At the end of each period: look over the totals for anything that seems off, such as a missing month of rent or a duplicated expense.
  • Before the deadline: send the update. Most compatible software shows you the summary before you submit.

If you use an accountant, agree who sends the updates. Some people keep the records and let their accountant submit; others do the whole thing themselves.

Choosing software that makes this easy

The effort quarterly updates take depends heavily on your software. Software that creates digital records can pull in bank transactions and let you snap receipts, which cuts down manual entry. Bridging software works with spreadsheets you already keep and sends the figures to HMRC.

GOV.UK suggests checking that any product supports all your income sources and can submit your tax return as well as quarterly updates.

Whatever you choose, confirm it is listed in HMRC's software finder for MTD for Income Tax.

Penalties for late updates

HMRC has said it will not give penalty points for late quarterly updates in the 2026 to 2027 tax year, the first year of MTD for Income Tax. That covers the first group of users. The rules for later years and later groups are on GOV.UK's "Send quarterly updates" guidance, so check what applies to your start year rather than assuming the same leniency.

The end of the year

Quarterly updates do not replace your end-of-year obligations. After the tax year ends you still finalise your figures and submit your tax return through your software. The quarterly updates simply mean most of the work is already done.
