MTD Ready

Moving from spreadsheets to MTD software: a step-by-step plan

Plenty of sole traders and landlords run their finances on a spreadsheet, and it works. Making Tax Digital (MTD) for Income Tax does not ban spreadsheets, but it does mean your spreadsheet alone is no longer enough to report to HMRC. This guide sets out your two options and a step-by-step plan for whichever you choose.

Can I keep using my spreadsheet?

Yes, with the right software alongside it. GOV.UK describes two kinds of MTD-compatible software:

  • Bridging software, which connects to spreadsheets you already keep and sends your figures to HMRC.
  • Software that creates your digital records, such as by connecting to your bank, scanning receipts or manual entry, and then builds your quarterly updates itself.

So the real question is not whether to give up your spreadsheet, but whether you want to.

Option 1: keep the spreadsheet and add bridging software

This suits you if:

  • your spreadsheet is tidy and kept up to date
  • you have a modest number of transactions
  • you are comfortable with how it works and do not want to change

The trade-off is that you are still doing all the data entry yourself. Bridging software sends figures; it does not record your transactions for you.

Steps for this route

  1. Tidy the spreadsheet. Make sure income and expenses are clearly separated, by business if you have more than one (for example self-employment and property).
  2. Check category headings. Your figures need to map to the categories HMRC expects. Bridging products usually explain how to set this up.
  3. Pick a bridging product that supports all your income sources and your accounting period, and can submit your tax return as well as quarterly updates.
  4. Do a trial run with a past period's figures, so you know the link between spreadsheet and software works before a real deadline.

Option 2: move to record-keeping software

This suits you if:

  • your spreadsheet is often weeks behind
  • you have lots of transactions or receipts
  • you would rather spend less time on admin each month

The trade-off is a learning curve and, for many products, a subscription. GOV.UK notes that free options exist for simple tax affairs, so check those too.

Steps for this route

  1. Shortlist two or three products that support all your income sources. If you have property and self-employment income, that rules out many.
  2. Open a separate business bank account if you do not have one. Bank connections work far better when business and personal spending are not mixed.
  3. Set up the business details in your chosen software, including each income source as a separate business.
  4. Bring in opening figures for the current tax year so far, either by importing from your spreadsheet or entering totals.
  5. Run both systems side by side for a month or two. Check the software's totals match your spreadsheet. When they agree, retire the spreadsheet.

Whichever product you look at, confirm it appears in HMRC's software finder for MTD for Income Tax.

How to choose between the two

A quick test: look at your spreadsheet right now. Is it up to date to within the last month?

  • Yes, and you enjoy keeping it: bridging software is the low-change option.
  • No, or it feels like a chore: record-keeping software will probably save you time once it is set up.

Remember quarterly updates are cumulative, covering the tax year to date. That suits both routes: if you correct something in your records, whether spreadsheet or software, the next update picks up the corrected totals.

When to switch

Do not leave the move until just before your first quarterly update. Your start date depends on your qualifying income, which is your self-employment and property income before expenses:

  • More than £30,000 in the 2025 to 2026 tax year: you start on 6 April 2027.
  • More than £20,000 in the 2026 to 2027 tax year: you start on 6 April 2028.

If April 2027 applies to you, switching during the winter gives you a few months to get comfortable before the first deadline of 7 August.

Common switching mistakes

Starting mid-quarter without opening balances. If you begin using software partway through a period, enter the year-to-date figures so your first update is complete.

Forgetting a second income source. A sole trader who also lets a room or a flat needs software that handles property income too.

Mixing personal and business transactions. This is the biggest source of messy records in both spreadsheets and software.

Assuming "MTD ready" means MTD for Income Tax. Some products were built for MTD for VAT, which is a different scheme. Check the product is listed for Income Tax.

What to bring across from your spreadsheet

Whichever route you take, gather these before you start:

  • Year-to-date totals for income and each expense category, for every income source.
  • A list of regular transactions, such as rent received, phone bills and insurance, so you can set up rules or categories once.
  • Receipts you still need to keep. Digital records do not change the need to keep evidence for your expenses.
  • Your accounting period, so you can check the software supports it.

Having these to hand turns setup into an afternoon's work rather than a week of searching.

A simple timeline

  • Now: decide between bridging and record-keeping software.
  • Next month: set up the software and enter opening figures.
  • The following one or two months: run it alongside your spreadsheet and compare.
  • Before your start date: sign up for MTD for Income Tax and authorise your software.
