MTD for Income Tax from April 2027: a checklist for sole traders

If you are a sole trader or landlord, Making Tax Digital (MTD) for Income Tax changes how you keep records and report to HMRC. The first group joined in April 2026. The next group joins on 6 April 2027, and it is much larger: anyone whose qualifying income is more than £30,000 in the 2025 to 2026 tax year.

This checklist walks through what to do between now and then, in order. Every rule below comes from GOV.UK guidance, and each section tells you where to check the detail for your own situation.

Step 1: Check whether the April 2027 date applies to you

The deadline depends on your qualifying income, and this is the point most people get wrong.

Qualifying income is your total income from self-employment and property before expenses. In other words, it is your turnover, not your profit. A plumber whose invoices add up to more than £30,000 a year is over the line, even if materials and van costs leave a much smaller profit.

What counts:

  • income from self-employment
  • income from property you let out
  • your share of income from jointly owned property

What does not count:

  • wages from employment taxed through PAYE
  • your share of profit as a partner in a partnership
  • dividends
  • the State Pension and private pensions

GOV.UK gives a worked example: £25,000 of rental income plus £27,000 of self-employment income makes £52,000 of qualifying income. Two people who own a property equally that earns £50,000 each count £25,000.

The phased dates are:

  • 6 April 2026: qualifying income over £50,000 in the 2024 to 2025 tax year
  • 6 April 2027: qualifying income over £30,000 in the 2025 to 2026 tax year
  • 6 April 2028: qualifying income over £20,000 in the 2026 to 2027 tax year

So the figure that decides your April 2027 start is the turnover on your 2025 to 2026 tax return. If you are close to the line, add up your self-employment and property income from that return before deciding you are exempt.

Step 2: Understand what will actually change

Under MTD for Income Tax you need to:

  1. keep digital records of your business income and expenses
  2. use compatible software to send HMRC a quarterly update every three months
  3. sign up for MTD for Income Tax before your start date

Quarterly updates sound daunting, but they are summaries of your income and expenses rather than full tax returns. They are also cumulative: each one covers the period from the start of the tax year to the end of the latest quarter. That means if you spot a mistake in an earlier quarter, you correct your records and the next update picks it up. You do not resubmit the earlier quarter.

The deadlines for the standard update periods are:

Update period Deadline
6 April to 5 July 7 August
6 April to 5 October 7 November
6 April to 5 January 7 February
6 April to 5 April 7 May

If your business runs on calendar quarters, you can use calendar update periods instead (1 April to 30 June, and so on). The deadlines are the same.

Step 3: Decide how you will keep digital records

This is the decision that matters most, because you will live with it every week. GOV.UK describes two kinds of compatible software:

  • Software that creates your digital records, typically by connecting to your bank account, letting you photograph receipts, or entering transactions by hand. The software then builds your quarterly updates from those records.
  • Bridging software, which connects to spreadsheets you already keep and sends the figures to HMRC.

Bridging software suits people with tidy spreadsheets and simple affairs. Record-keeping software suits people who would rather not maintain a spreadsheet at all, or who want their bank transactions pulled in automatically.

GOV.UK's advice is to use its software finder and check three things:

  • the software supports all your income sources, for example self-employment and property if you have both
  • it can send quarterly updates and submit your tax return
  • it supports your accounting period

GOV.UK also notes that free options exist for people with simple tax affairs, so do not assume you have to pay.

Whichever product you look at, confirm it appears in HMRC's software finder for MTD for Income Tax before you sign up.

Step 4: Get your records into shape now

You do not need to wait until April 2027 to start. Moving to digital records a few months early means your first real quarterly update will not also be your first week using new software.

A practical order of work:

  1. Separate business and personal spending if you have not already. A dedicated bank account makes digital records far easier.
  2. Pick your software and set it up with your business details.
  3. Run it alongside your current method for a month or two, so you can compare totals and spot gaps.
  4. Decide how you will capture receipts as they happen, rather than in a pile at the end of the year.
  5. Put the four quarterly deadlines in your calendar for the 2027 to 2028 tax year.

Step 5: Sign up before your start date

Signing up is a separate step from buying software. You must sign up for MTD for Income Tax before your start date, and you must authorise your chosen software to talk to HMRC. GOV.UK's "Check if you're eligible for Making Tax Digital for Income Tax" guidance explains how. If you use an accountant, ask whether they will sign you up as your agent.

Step 6: Know what is the same, and where there is breathing room

Some things do not change. You keep submitting your normal Self Assessment return until your MTD requirement begins.

HMRC has also said it will not give penalty points for late quarterly updates in the 2026 to 2027 tax year. That applies to the first group. Do not rely on similar leniency for the April 2027 group; check the "Send quarterly updates" guidance on GOV.UK for the rules that apply to your start year.

Quick checklist

  • ☐ Add up self-employment and property turnover from your 2025 to 2026 return
  • ☐ Over £30,000? Your start date is 6 April 2027
  • ☐ Choose record-keeping or bridging software that supports all your income sources
  • ☐ Check the product is in HMRC's software finder
  • ☐ Start keeping digital records early and run them in parallel
  • ☐ Sign up for MTD for Income Tax and authorise your software
  • ☐ Diary the four quarterly deadlines

This guide is general information, not tax advice. If your situation is unusual, for example you have several businesses or jointly owned property, check the GOV.UK guidance or speak to an accountant.
