MTD Ready

Do sole traders need a business bank account?

The short answer

No. According to official UK government business guidance, sole traders and ordinary partnerships are not legally required to have a business bank account. You can legally run your business through your personal current account.

The same guidance says that keeping personal and business money separate makes accounting simpler, for three reasons:

  • Business transactions are easier to find. You are not searching through groceries, subscriptions and family transfers to find a client payment or a supplier invoice.
  • Cash flow is easier to track. You can see what the business has coming in and going out.
  • Tax records are simpler. Your records start from a cleaner set of transactions.

So the decision is practical rather than legal. The rest of this guide explains why that practical choice matters more once Making Tax Digital (MTD) for Income Tax applies to you.

Why this matters more under MTD for Income Tax

MTD for Income Tax changes how many sole traders and landlords keep records and report to HMRC. If you are within MTD for Income Tax, you must:

  1. Keep digital records of your income and expenses.
  2. Use compatible software to send quarterly updates of your income and expenses to HMRC.
  3. Sign up before your start date.

Under MTD you are reporting income and expenses through the year, so a cleaner set of transactions can save effort each quarter. A bank account does not keep your digital records for you. However, if every transaction in an account belongs to the business, you have less to sort before you record or check it.

When MTD for Income Tax applies

MTD for Income Tax is being phased in according to your qualifying income:

Start date Applies if your qualifying income was more than In the tax year
6 April 2026 £50,000 2024 to 2025
6 April 2027 £30,000 2025 to 2026
6 April 2028 £20,000 2026 to 2027

To confirm whether these rules apply to you, use the eligibility checker on GOV.UK.

What counts as qualifying income

Qualifying income is your total income from self-employment and property before expenses. In other words, it is your turnover, not your profit.

These types of income do not count:

  • Employment (PAYE) income
  • Your share of profit from a partnership
  • Dividends
  • The State Pension
  • Private pensions

If you own property jointly, only your share counts.

GOV.UK gives two examples:

  • Someone with £25,000 of rental income and £27,000 of self-employment income has £52,000 of qualifying income.
  • A jointly owned property earning £50,000, owned equally by two people, gives each owner £25,000 of qualifying income.

The thresholds are based on income before expenses. That means your total business takings need to be easy to see, which is another reason a dedicated account can help.

How quarterly updates work

Under the standard periods, the four quarterly updates and their deadlines are:

Update period Deadline
6 April to 5 July 7 August
6 April to 5 October 7 November
6 April to 5 January 7 February
6 April to 5 April 7 May

If you choose calendar periods, the periods end on 30 June, 30 September, 31 December and 31 March. The deadlines are the same.

Two points from GOV.UK are worth knowing:

  • Quarterly updates are cumulative. Each one covers the period from the start of the tax year to the end of the update period. If you find a mistake in an earlier quarter, you can correct your records without resubmitting that earlier update.
  • Quarterly updates are summaries, not tax returns.

HMRC has also said it will not give penalty points for late quarterly updates in the 2026 to 2027 tax year. Even so, regular record-keeping will make each update quicker. Keeping business transactions in their own account is one simple way to support that habit.

What about landlords?

The government guidance on business bank accounts covers sole traders and ordinary partnerships. It does not say whether landlords need a separate account for rental income.

If you let property, check GOV.UK for any requirements that apply to your situation. Also check any terms set by a mortgage lender or letting arrangement.

Property income does count towards your MTD qualifying income. So the same practical point applies. If rent and property costs go through their own account, that money is easier to identify when you keep digital records and prepare quarterly updates.

Traditional banks and app-based providers

If you decide to open a separate account, the government guidance describes two broad options.

Traditional banks - Can take longer to set up - May offer branches, overdrafts and loans

App-based providers - Can usually be set up online quickly - May offer limited overdrafts

Neither option is right for everyone. Consider these questions:

  • Do you handle cash or cheques? If so, branch access may matter to you.
  • Might you need borrowing? If you expect to need an overdraft or a loan, check what each provider offers.
  • How quickly do you need the account? A fast online set-up may suit you if you are starting out or reorganising your finances before MTD applies.
  • How do you plan to keep your MTD records? If you intend to use MTD software, check whether that software can work with the account you are considering. Ask the software provider or the bank directly. Do not assume it will connect.

Check fees, eligibility and features on each provider's own website before you apply. These can vary and may change.

Where a business current account such as Tide could fit

You may prefer an app-based account that you can open online and keep separate from your personal banking. Tide offers a business current account aimed at small businesses, including sole traders.

As with any provider, check the current fees, the eligibility criteria and any overdraft or lending options before you apply. If you plan to use MTD-compatible software, confirm separately that the software you choose works with the account.

If you keep using one account

Using a personal account for business is allowed. If you do this, these habits can make your MTD records easier to manage:

  • Record business transactions regularly in your digital records. Recording little and often is easier than working through everything before a deadline.
  • Use clear references on business payments in and out.
  • Avoid mixing business and personal spending on the same transaction where you can.
  • Review your records before each quarterly deadline. Remember that updates are cumulative, so you can correct earlier entries.

Choosing MTD software

Whichever bank account you use, you need compatible software if you are within MTD for Income Tax. GOV.UK recommends that you:

  • Use the software finder on GOV.UK.
  • Choose between software that creates digital records and bridging software that works with spreadsheets.
  • Check that the software supports your income sources (for example, self-employment, property or both) and your accounting period.

GOV.UK also notes that free options exist for simple tax affairs.

Getting help from an accountant or bookkeeper

You do not have to manage MTD on your own. Under MTD for Income Tax:

  • You can have one main agent at a time. A main agent can fully manage your MTD for Income Tax.
  • You can have any number of supporting agents. A supporting agent has limited access and can only see your business and property income details.
  • An agent, such as an accountant or bookkeeper, can sign you up with your permission.

If you use an agent, ask what bank and record-keeping set-up they find easiest to work with. A separate account often makes the review process simpler for everyone involved.

Quick checklist

  • ☐ Work out your qualifying income: self-employment plus property, before expenses, counting only your share of jointly owned property.
  • ☐ Check your MTD start date on GOV.UK.
  • ☐ Decide whether to open a separate business account. It is not legally required for sole traders, but it can simplify records.
  • ☐ Compare a traditional bank with an app-based provider on set-up time, branch access and borrowing.
  • ☐ Choose compatible software using the GOV.UK software finder.
  • ☐ Sign up for MTD for Income Tax before your start date, or ask an agent to sign you up.
  • ☐ Note your quarterly deadlines: 7 August, 7 November, 7 February and 7 May.
