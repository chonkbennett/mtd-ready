Employed with a side business? How MTD for Income Tax applies

The short answer

If you have a job taxed through PAYE and also run a side business or let out property, your salary does not count towards the Making Tax Digital (MTD) for Income Tax threshold. HMRC only looks at your qualifying income, which is your total income from self-employment and property before expenses (your turnover).

Many employed people with a side business will therefore fall below the threshold for longer than they might expect. Others, especially those with both a side business and rental income, may reach it sooner than they think, because those two sources are added together.

This guide covers how to work out where you stand, when MTD could apply to you, and what you would need to do.

What counts as qualifying income

GOV.UK defines qualifying income as your total income from self-employment and property before expenses.

These do not count:

  • employment (PAYE) income
  • your share of partnership profit
  • dividends
  • the State Pension
  • private pensions

For jointly owned property, only your share counts.

Two details catch people out:

  1. It is turnover, not profit. Turnover means the money coming in before you deduct any expenses. A side business with high costs and small profits can still have qualifying income above the threshold.
  2. Self-employment and property income are combined. If you are employed, run a small business on the side and also let out a property, add the business turnover and the rental income together.

The GOV.UK examples

GOV.UK gives this example: £25,000 of rental income plus £27,000 of self-employment income gives £52,000 of qualifying income.

For joint ownership, a property earning £50,000 that is owned equally gives each owner £25,000 of qualifying income.

How this looks if you also have a job

Suppose you earn a salary of £45,000 through PAYE and your side business has turnover of £15,000. Your salary is ignored, so your qualifying income is £15,000. This is an illustration only, not a figure HMRC uses.

For example, if that same person also received £10,000 a year in rent from a property they own outright, their qualifying income would be £25,000. That is £15,000 from the business plus £10,000 from the property, with the salary still excluded.

When MTD for Income Tax starts

MTD for Income Tax is being introduced in stages. Each stage depends on your qualifying income in an earlier tax year.

MTD required from If qualifying income was more than In the tax year
6 April 2026 £50,000 2024 to 2025
6 April 2027 £30,000 2025 to 2026
6 April 2028 £20,000 2026 to 2027

To check where you stand:

  1. Find your self-employment turnover and your share of property income for the relevant tax year.
  2. Add them together.
  3. Leave out your salary and the other excluded income listed above.
  4. Compare the total with the threshold for that stage.

If you are below the thresholds now, check again each year. Your qualifying income may grow as your side business does. For your own circumstances, including any exemptions, use HMRC's eligibility checker on GOV.UK.

What you must do if MTD applies to you

According to GOV.UK, people within MTD for Income Tax must:

  • keep digital records of their self-employment and property income and expenses
  • use compatible software to send quarterly updates of income and expenses to HMRC
  • sign up before their start date

MTD covers your business and property income. This guide does not have verified details on how your employment income is reported at the end of the year under MTD, so check GOV.UK for how your PAYE income fits into your year-end tax position.

Quarterly updates: periods and deadlines

If you use standard periods, your quarterly updates cover:

Update period Deadline
6 April to 5 July 7 August
6 April to 5 October 7 November
6 April to 5 January 7 February
6 April to 5 April 7 May

You can choose calendar periods instead. These end on 30 June, 30 September, 31 December and 31 March, starting from 1 April. The deadlines are the same.

Some points about quarterly updates:

  • They are cumulative. Each update covers the period from the start of the tax year to the end of the update period. If you spot a mistake in an earlier quarter, you can correct it in a later update without resubmitting the earlier one.
  • They are summaries, not tax returns. They give HMRC an ongoing picture of your income and expenses.
  • There is a soft start for late updates. HMRC will not give penalty points for late quarterly updates for the 2026 to 2027 tax year. It is still sensible to build the habit from the start.

If you have a full-time job, the quarterly rhythm matters. You need a routine that fits around your working week, such as recording transactions weekly rather than leaving everything until the deadline.

Keeping side-business money separate

Sole traders are not legally required to have a business bank account. However, GOV.UK notes that separating personal and business finances makes accounting simpler:

  • business transactions are easier to find
  • cash flow is easier to track
  • records for tax are simpler

This matters if your salary and your side-business income currently land in the same account. Under MTD, you will be summarising business income and expenses every quarter, so a separate account can save you from sorting through personal spending each time.

When choosing a provider:

  • Traditional banks can take longer to set up but may offer branches, overdrafts and loans.
  • App-based providers set up online quickly but may offer limited overdrafts.

Choosing software

GOV.UK suggests three steps:

  1. Use the GOV.UK software finder to see which products are compatible with MTD for Income Tax.
  2. Choose the type of software that suits how you work. Some software creates digital records. Bridging software works with the spreadsheets you already keep.
  3. Check the software supports your income sources and accounting period. For example, confirm it handles self-employment and property income if you have both.

GOV.UK also notes that free options exist for simple tax affairs. If your side business is small and straightforward, it is worth checking these before paying for anything.

If you would rather have full bookkeeping software that keeps your digital records, Xero is one option to consider. Check on the GOV.UK software finder that it supports your particular mix of income sources and your accounting period before you commit. For transparency, Xero runs a free-to-join affiliate programme.

Getting help from an accountant or bookkeeper

You do not have to manage MTD alone. Under MTD for Income Tax, you can have:

  • one main agent at a time, who can fully manage your MTD for Income Tax
  • any number of supporting agents, who have limited access and can only see your business and property income details

An agent, such as an accountant or bookkeeper, can sign you up with your permission. This can be useful if your job leaves little time for paperwork. For example, you could have an accountant as your main agent and a bookkeeper as a supporting agent who handles your quarterly records.

A simple checklist for employed people with a side business

  1. Add up your qualifying income. Include self-employment turnover and your share of property income, before expenses. Leave out your salary.
  2. Compare it with the thresholds. The stages start in 2026, 2027 and 2028, and each depends on an earlier tax year.
  3. Recheck each year as your side income changes.
  4. Consider a separate bank account for side-business money.
  5. Choose compatible software using the GOV.UK software finder.
  6. Sign up before your start date, either yourself or through an agent.
  7. Set a quarterly routine that works around your employment.

For anything not covered here, including exemptions, how to sign up and how your employment income is handled at the end of the year, check the latest guidance on GOV.UK.
