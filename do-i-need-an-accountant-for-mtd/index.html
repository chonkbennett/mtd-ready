MTD Ready

Do I need an accountant for MTD for Income Tax?

If you're a sole trader or landlord, you may be wondering whether Making Tax Digital (MTD) for Income Tax means you now need an accountant. GOV.UK describes what you must do: keep digital records, use compatible software and send quarterly updates. It does not say you must use an accountant. You can meet these requirements yourself, or an agent such as an accountant or bookkeeper can handle them for you.

This guide covers what MTD asks of you, which parts you can manage on your own, and when professional help may be worth considering.

First, check whether MTD for Income Tax applies to you

MTD for Income Tax depends on your qualifying income. This is your total income from self-employment and property before expenses, which is your turnover rather than your profit.

GOV.UK sets out these start dates:

You must use MTD from If your qualifying income was more than In the tax year
6 April 2026 £50,000 2024 to 2025
6 April 2027 £30,000 2025 to 2026
6 April 2028 £20,000 2026 to 2027

Some income does not count towards qualifying income:

  • employment (PAYE) income
  • your share of partnership profit
  • dividends
  • the State Pension and private pensions

If you own property jointly, only your share of the income counts.

GOV.UK examples:

  • If you have £25,000 of rental income and £27,000 of self-employment income, your qualifying income is £52,000. That is above the £50,000 threshold.
  • If a jointly owned property earns £50,000 and is owned equally, each owner has £25,000 of qualifying income from it.

Many people only realise they need help at this stage. They may have both rental and trading income, or a jointly owned property, and are unsure whether they are over a threshold. If your situation is unclear, check the GOV.UK guidance on working out qualifying income or ask an adviser before you assume either way.

What MTD for Income Tax actually asks you to do

According to GOV.UK, if you are within MTD for Income Tax you must:

  1. Keep digital records of your business and property income and expenses.
  2. Use compatible software to send quarterly updates of income and expenses to HMRC.
  3. Sign up before your start date.

There are also end-of-year obligations. The details of these are not covered here, so check GOV.UK for what you need to submit after the tax year ends and when.

Quarterly update deadlines

Using standard periods, the quarters and deadlines are:

Update period Deadline
6 April to 5 July 7 August
6 April to 5 October 7 November
6 April to 5 January 7 February
6 April to 5 April 7 May

You can choose calendar periods instead (quarters ending 30 June, 30 September, 31 December and 31 March). The deadlines are the same.

Two points from GOV.UK make these updates less daunting than they might seem:

  • Updates are cumulative. Each one covers the period from the start of the tax year to the end of that quarter. If you find a mistake from an earlier quarter, you can correct it in a later update without resubmitting the earlier one.
  • Updates are summaries, not tax returns. They report your income and expenses for the period. They are not a final calculation of what you owe.

HMRC has also said it will not give penalty points for late quarterly updates in the 2026 to 2027 tax year. This gives you time to get used to the process, but it is still wise to build the habit of meeting deadlines from the start.

What you can realistically handle yourself

Many sole traders and landlords with straightforward affairs can manage MTD on their own. GOV.UK suggests this approach to choosing software:

  • Use the GOV.UK software finder to see compatible products.
  • Choose the type of software that suits you. One type creates and stores your digital records. The other, called bridging software, connects your existing spreadsheets to HMRC.
  • Check that the software supports your income sources (for example, self-employment, UK property or both) and your accounting period.
  • Look at free options if your tax affairs are simple. GOV.UK notes that free software exists for simple cases.

Doing it yourself tends to suit you if:

  • you have one income source, or a simple mix
  • you already keep tidy records, perhaps in a spreadsheet you are happy to keep using with bridging software
  • you are comfortable with software and with checking your own figures
  • you are confident about which expenses you can claim (if you're unsure, check GOV.UK)

A simple step that helps either way: separate your finances

GOV.UK says sole traders are not legally required to have a business bank account. It does note that keeping personal and business money separate makes accounting simpler:

  • business transactions are easier to find
  • cash flow is easier to track
  • your tax records are simpler

When choosing an account, GOV.UK notes that traditional banks can take longer to set up but may offer branches, overdrafts and loans. App-based providers are quick to set up online but may offer limited overdrafts. Whichever you choose, separate finances make digital record-keeping easier, whether you do it yourself or pass it to an accountant.

When an accountant may be worth considering

An accountant is not required, but they can be useful in some situations. You might consider professional help if:

  • You're unsure whether you're in scope. Mixed income, joint ownership or income near a threshold can make qualifying income harder to work out.
  • You have several income sources. For example, you might run a trade and also let property, and want everything handled in one place.
  • You don't want to manage software or deadlines. Some people prefer to hand over the quarterly routine completely.
  • You want help with the end-of-year position. Quarterly updates are summaries, so you may want someone to check that your overall tax position is correct.
  • You'd like someone to sign you up. GOV.UK confirms that an agent, such as an accountant or bookkeeper, can sign a client up for MTD for Income Tax with their permission.

If any of these sound familiar, an online service may be a practical middle ground between doing everything yourself and using a traditional practice.

How agents work under MTD for Income Tax

If you do use professional help, it is useful to know how HMRC handles agent access. Under MTD for Income Tax, you can have:

  • one main agent at a time. A main agent can fully manage your MTD for Income Tax.
  • any number of supporting agents. A supporting agent has limited access and can only see your business and property income details.

This means you can mix and match. For example, an accountant could act as your main agent while a bookkeeper acts as a supporting agent. Or you could keep control of your quarterly updates yourself and bring in help only where you need it.

Questions to ask before choosing an accountant

If you decide to look for help, these questions can help you compare options:

  1. Will you act as my main agent or a supporting agent?
  2. Can you sign me up for MTD for Income Tax before my start date?
  3. Which software will we use, and does it support all my income sources and my accounting period?
  4. Who is responsible for sending each quarterly update, and when will you need my records?
  5. Will you handle my end-of-year obligations too?
  6. Do you work with landlords, sole traders, or both, depending on what I need?
  7. What exactly is included in your fee, and what costs extra?

A quick decision checklist

You may be fine on your own if:

  • you're clearly above or below the relevant threshold
  • you have simple records and one main income source
  • you've found compatible software (possibly free) that supports your income type
  • you're comfortable correcting figures in later cumulative updates

You may benefit from an accountant if:

  • your qualifying income is borderline or hard to work out
  • you have joint property, multiple income sources or complex expenses
  • you would rather not manage software, sign-up and deadlines yourself
  • you want reassurance about your end-of-year tax position

The bottom line

You do not need an accountant to comply with MTD for Income Tax. What you do need is digital records, compatible software, quarterly updates sent on time and sign-up before your start date. Many sole traders and landlords with simple affairs can manage this themselves using the GOV.UK software finder.

An accountant can be useful if your income is complex, you're unsure whether you're in scope, or you'd rather someone else handle the process. Because MTD lets you have one main agent and several supporting agents, you can choose as much or as little help as suits you.
