MTD Ready

How to choose MTD-compatible software as a sole trader

If Making Tax Digital (MTD) for Income Tax applies to you, you will need compatible software. HMRC does not supply its own, so the choice is yours, and it is the decision you will feel every week once quarterly updates start.

This guide sets out what the software has to do, the two main types, and a short list of questions to ask before you pay for anything.

What the software must do

Under MTD for Income Tax, compatible software needs to let you:

  • keep digital records of your self-employment and property income and expenses
  • send quarterly updates to HMRC, the cumulative summaries due on 7 August, 7 November, 7 February and 7 May
  • submit your tax return at the end of the year

Some products only cover part of this. GOV.UK specifically advises checking that a product can both send quarterly updates and submit your tax return, so you are not juggling two systems.

The two types of software

GOV.UK splits compatible products into two groups.

Software that creates your digital records

This is full record-keeping software. Depending on the product, you might:

  • connect your business bank account so transactions flow in automatically
  • photograph receipts on your phone
  • type in income and expenses by hand

The software then totals your records and builds each quarterly update for you. This suits people who do not already keep good spreadsheets, or who would rather spend less time on admin each week.

Bridging software

Bridging software connects to spreadsheets you already keep and sends the figures to HMRC in the right format. It suits people with simple affairs and well-organised spreadsheets who want to keep working the way they do now.

Neither type is right for everyone. The question is how you keep records today and how much you want that to change.

Six questions to ask before you choose

1. Does it support all my income sources? If you are a sole trader and a landlord, the product must handle both self-employment and property income. Check this first; it rules out more products than anything else.

2. Does it send quarterly updates and submit the tax return? As above, you want one product that does both.

3. Does it support my accounting period? GOV.UK lists this as a key check. If your business does not run on the tax year, confirm the software can cope.

4. What does it cost, and is there a free option? GOV.UK notes that free software exists for people with simple tax affairs. Paid products usually add things like bank connections, receipt capture and invoicing. Work out which of those you would actually use.

5. Will my accountant work with it? If you have an accountant, ask which products they support. Some people have more than one agent, and GOV.UK suggests checking whether the software allows that.

6. Does it have HMRC Assist built in? GOV.UK mentions this as an extra to look for. It helps flag errors before you submit.

A simple way to decide

  • Simple affairs, good spreadsheets, low budget: start by looking at bridging software and free options.
  • Busy business, lots of transactions, little time: record-keeping software with bank connections usually saves the most effort.
  • Sole trader and landlord, or several businesses: narrow the list to products that clearly support every income source, then compare.

Mistakes people make when choosing

Choosing on price alone. The cheapest product is fine if it covers everything you need. If it does not support your property income, or cannot submit the tax return, you will end up paying for a second tool or redoing work.

Forgetting about the second income. Plenty of sole traders also let a room, a flat or a garage. If that income is part of your qualifying income, your software needs to handle property as well as self-employment.

Assuming spreadsheets are no longer allowed. They are. Bridging software exists precisely so that people who like their spreadsheets can keep them, as long as the spreadsheet holds proper digital records and the bridging product sends the figures to HMRC.

Leaving the decision until the deadline. Quarterly updates are short, but the first one is much easier if your records are already in the software and you know where everything is.

Not checking the official finder. Marketing pages can say "MTD ready" about many things, including MTD for VAT, which is a different scheme. Confirm the product is listed for MTD for Income Tax specifically.

Give yourself time to switch

The worst time to learn new software is the week a deadline is due. If your start date is 6 April 2027, aim to:

  1. shortlist two or three products using the questions above
  2. try your favourite with a few weeks of real transactions before committing
  3. pick one and move your records over well before your first quarterly update

Because quarterly updates are cumulative, mistakes in early quarters can be corrected in your records and picked up in the next update. Starting early still makes the first year far less stressful.

Where to find the official list

GOV.UK's page "Find software that's compatible with Making Tax Digital for Income Tax" includes HMRC's software finder. You answer a few questions about your income and it shows matching products. Use it as your final check before you sign up for anything.
