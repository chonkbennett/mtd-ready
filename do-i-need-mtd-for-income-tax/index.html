Do I need Making Tax Digital for Income Tax? How to check

Most of the confusion about Making Tax Digital (MTD) for Income Tax comes down to one question: does it apply to me, and from when? You can answer that in about ten minutes with your last tax return in front of you. This guide shows you how.

The short answer

MTD for Income Tax applies to sole traders and landlords once their qualifying income passes a threshold. It is being introduced in three stages, and the threshold falls each year:

You must use MTD from If your qualifying income is more than Measured in the tax year
6 April 2026 £50,000 2024 to 2025
6 April 2027 £30,000 2025 to 2026
6 April 2028 £20,000 2026 to 2027

Notice the gap between the two columns. The tax year that decides whether you are in is the one that ended before your start date. For the April 2027 stage, HMRC looks at your 2025 to 2026 figures.

What "qualifying income" means

This is the part to get right, because it is not the number most people expect.

Qualifying income is your total income from self-employment and property before expenses. It is your turnover, or gross takings, not your profit.

If you are a hairdresser with high takings but heavy salon costs, or a landlord whose mortgage interest and repairs swallow much of the rent, your profit might look small. MTD ignores that. It adds up the money coming in from self-employment and property before anything is taken off.

Income that counts

  • Self-employed income, from any number of sole trader businesses
  • Income from property you let out, in the UK or overseas
  • Your share of income from property you own jointly

Income that does not count

  • Wages or salary from a job taxed through PAYE
  • Your share of profit as a partner in a business partnership
  • Dividends from shares
  • The State Pension
  • Private and workplace pensions

So someone with a full-time job and a small side business only counts the side business. A retired person with a pension and a buy-to-let only counts the rent.

Two worked examples from GOV.UK

Mixed income. You receive £25,000 in rent and earn £27,000 from self-employment. Neither figure is over £30,000 on its own, but together your qualifying income is £52,000. Combined income over the threshold is what matters.

Joint property. Two people own a let property equally, and it brings in £50,000 a year. Each owner counts their share, so each has £25,000 of qualifying income from it, plus anything else they earn from self-employment or other property.

How to check your own position, step by step

  1. Find your 2025 to 2026 Self Assessment return (or your figures for that year, if you have not filed yet).
  2. Write down your self-employment turnover. That is the total income figure for each business, before expenses. Add them together if you have more than one.
  3. Write down your property income, again before expenses. For jointly owned property, use your share only.
  4. Add the two totals together. That is your qualifying income.
  5. Compare it with the table above. More than £30,000 means you start on 6 April 2027.

If your total is under £30,000 but over £20,000, you are not off the hook: repeat the exercise for the 2026 to 2027 tax year, because the next stage starts on 6 April 2028.

If MTD does apply to you

Once you are in, three things change:

  • You keep digital records of your business income and expenses.
  • You use compatible software to send HMRC a summary every quarter. These quarterly updates are cumulative, so each one covers the tax year to date.
  • You sign up for MTD for Income Tax before your start date and authorise your software.

The quarterly deadlines are 7 August, 7 November, 7 February and 7 May.

Choosing software is the step that takes the most thought. GOV.UK suggests deciding first whether you want software that creates the records for you, or bridging software that works with spreadsheets you already keep. Then check that the product supports all your income sources and your accounting period. Free options exist for simple tax affairs.

Before you commit to any product, check it is listed in HMRC's software finder for MTD for Income Tax.

Common questions

My profit is under £30,000. Am I exempt? Not necessarily. The test uses income before expenses. Check your turnover, not your profit.

I have a job and a small side business. Does my salary count? No. PAYE employment income is excluded. Only the side business, and any property income, counts.

I am a partner in a partnership. Does that count? Your share of partnership profit as an individual partner does not count towards qualifying income.

Can I join early? GOV.UK allows some people to sign up voluntarily before they have to. If you are going to be in the April 2027 group anyway, starting early with digital records gives you time to get used to the software.

Where to check the detail

GOV.UK has two pages that cover this: "Check if you're eligible for Making Tax Digital for Income Tax" and "Work out your qualifying income for Making Tax Digital for Income Tax". The second covers less common cases this guide does not, such as income from overseas.
