Bridging software for MTD: keeping your spreadsheet

The short answer

You may not have to give up your spreadsheet when Making Tax Digital (MTD) for Income Tax applies to you. GOV.UK says you can choose between two types of compatible software:

  • Software that creates digital records, such as full accounting packages.
  • Bridging software that works with spreadsheets, letting you keep your records in a spreadsheet and send the required information to HMRC.

If your spreadsheet already works well and your tax affairs are straightforward, bridging software may be all you need. This guide covers who MTD for Income Tax applies to, what you must do, how bridging software fits in, and when moving to full accounting software might make more sense.

Does MTD for Income Tax apply to you?

MTD for Income Tax is being phased in according to your qualifying income:

Start date Applies if qualifying income was more than In the tax year
6 April 2026 £50,000 2024 to 2025
6 April 2027 £30,000 2025 to 2026
6 April 2028 £20,000 2026 to 2027

Working out your qualifying income

Qualifying income is your total income from self-employment and property before expenses. In other words, it is your turnover, not your profit.

These do not count towards qualifying income:

  • Employment (PAYE) income
  • A partner's share of partnership profit
  • Dividends
  • The State Pension
  • Private pensions

If you own property jointly, only your share counts.

GOV.UK gives two examples:

  • Someone with £25,000 of rental income and £27,000 of self-employment income has £52,000 of qualifying income. That is over the £50,000 threshold.
  • A jointly owned property earning £50,000, owned equally by two people, gives each owner £25,000 of qualifying income.

The second example matters for landlords. A property with a large rental income may not bring each owner into MTD at the first stage, but each owner should check their own position against the later thresholds.

If your situation is not covered here, for example unusual ownership splits or income types not listed above, check GOV.UK's guidance on working out qualifying income.

What MTD for Income Tax asks you to do

Once you are within MTD for Income Tax, you must:

  1. Keep digital records of your income and expenses.
  2. Use compatible software to send quarterly updates of income and expenses to HMRC.
  3. Sign up before your start date.

A spreadsheet can be your digital record. The difference under MTD is that you must use compatible software to send your updates to HMRC. Bridging software provides that link.

For detailed rules on how a spreadsheet and bridging software must connect, check the current guidance on GOV.UK.

Quarterly updates: periods and deadlines

Standard quarterly periods

Period Deadline
6 April to 5 July 7 August
6 April to 5 October 7 November
6 April to 5 January 7 February
6 April to 5 April 7 May

Calendar quarterly periods

You can instead use calendar periods ending 30 June, 30 September, 31 December and 31 March. The deadlines are the same: 7 August, 7 November, 7 February and 7 May.

Two points that make quarterly updates easier

Updates are cumulative. Each update covers the period from the start of the tax year to the end of that quarter. If you find a mistake in an earlier quarter, you can correct it in your records and it will flow into your next update. You do not need to resubmit earlier quarters.

Updates are summaries, not tax returns. You are sending a summary of income and expenses, not calculating your final tax bill each quarter. This reduces the pressure on each submission.

Penalty points in the first year

HMRC will not give penalty points for late quarterly updates for the 2026 to 2027 tax year. That gives people joining from April 2026 a chance to get used to the process. It is still sensible to aim for the deadlines from the start so the routine is in place for later years. For penalty rules after that year, check GOV.UK.

How bridging software works with your spreadsheet

Bridging software sits between your spreadsheet and HMRC. You keep recording income and expenses in your spreadsheet as you do now. When an update is due, the bridging software takes the relevant figures from your spreadsheet and sends them to HMRC.

Who bridging software tends to suit

  • Sole traders or landlords with a simple, well-kept spreadsheet.
  • People with relatively few transactions each quarter.
  • Anyone who wants to keep their current process, at least for the first year of MTD.

Possible drawbacks

  • You still do the bookkeeping by hand. Bridging software sends figures. It does not usually make record-keeping easier.
  • Your spreadsheet must be laid out in a way the software can read. You may need to restructure it to fit.
  • Errors in the spreadsheet carry through. A wrong formula or a missing row will affect what you send.

Choosing compatible software: a checklist

GOV.UK recommends using its software finder to find compatible products. When you compare options:

  1. Decide which type you need. Choose between software that creates digital records and bridging software that works with spreadsheets.
  2. Check it supports your income sources. If you have self-employment and property income, make sure the software handles both.
  3. Check it supports your accounting period. Confirm it works with the dates you use.
  4. Consider free options. GOV.UK notes free options exist for people with simple tax affairs.
  5. Look at how it fits your routine. For bridging software, check how it connects to your type of spreadsheet. For full software, consider how much of your record-keeping you would be moving.

Only choose software that appears on GOV.UK's list of compatible products. Check the software finder for the current list rather than relying on a product's own marketing.

When to consider moving off the spreadsheet

Bridging software lets you keep what you know. However, some people find MTD a good moment to review their whole set-up. Full accounting software may be worth considering if:

  • Your spreadsheet has become large, slow or hard to check.
  • You spend a lot of time typing in bank transactions by hand.
  • You have several properties or income streams to keep separate.
  • You expect your record-keeping to grow over the next few years.

Xero is one accounting package UK sole traders and landlords may look at if they decide to move from a spreadsheet to software that creates digital records. Before choosing it, or any other product, check on GOV.UK's software finder that it supports your income sources and accounting period.

You do not have to decide everything at once. Some people start with bridging software to meet the first deadlines, then review whether full software would save time once they have seen a few quarters of the process.

A simple plan to get ready

  1. Work out your qualifying income for the relevant tax year, using turnover rather than profit and only your share of jointly owned property.
  2. Find your start date from the thresholds table above.
  3. Tidy your spreadsheet so income and expenses are clearly categorised and up to date.
  4. Choose compatible software using GOV.UK's software finder: bridging software if you are keeping your spreadsheet, or full accounting software if you are moving away from it.
  5. Sign up for MTD for Income Tax before your start date.
  6. Put the quarterly deadlines in your calendar: 7 August, 7 November, 7 February and 7 May.

If you are deciding between keeping your spreadsheet and switching, it can help to compare a full package with your current process before your first quarter begins.

Summary

MTD for Income Tax does not automatically mean giving up your spreadsheet. If your records are well organised and your affairs are simple, bridging software can send your quarterly updates to HMRC while you keep working the way you do now. If your spreadsheet is becoming hard to manage, MTD may be a good time to consider full accounting software. Whichever route you take, confirm your start date, check the software on GOV.UK's software finder, and sign up before you need to begin.
