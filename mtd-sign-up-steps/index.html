Signing up for MTD for Income Tax: the steps in order

Making Tax Digital (MTD) for Income Tax changes how many sole traders and landlords keep records and report to HMRC. Signing up is only one part of the process. If you do it before checking whether you need to, when you need to, and which software you will use, you may have to go back and redo steps.

This guide sets out a sensible order, from working out whether MTD applies to you through to sending your first quarterly update.

Step 1: Work out your qualifying income

Whether you need to join depends on your qualifying income. This is your total income from self-employment and property before expenses, which means your turnover, not your profit.

These types of income do not count:

  • employment (PAYE) income
  • your share of profit from a partnership
  • dividends
  • the State Pension
  • private pensions

If you jointly own a property, only your share of the income counts.

GOV.UK examples:

  • If you have £25,000 of rental income and £27,000 of self-employment income, your qualifying income is £52,000.
  • If a jointly owned property earns £50,000 and is owned equally by two people, each owner has £25,000 of qualifying income from it.

Add together the turnover from each self-employment and each property business you have, using your share where property is jointly owned.

Step 2: Find your start date

Once you know your qualifying income, compare it with the thresholds below. Each start date is based on your income in an earlier tax year.

You must use MTD for Income Tax from If your qualifying income was more than In the tax year
6 April 2026 £50,000 2024 to 2025
6 April 2027 £30,000 2025 to 2026
6 April 2028 £20,000 2026 to 2027

Using the first GOV.UK example above, qualifying income of £52,000 in the 2024 to 2025 tax year is more than £50,000. That person would need to use MTD for Income Tax from 6 April 2026.

If your income is below a threshold in one year, check the next threshold against the following tax year. Your start date could still come later. For exemptions, special circumstances, or questions about voluntary sign-up, check the eligibility guidance on GOV.UK.

Step 3: Understand what you will have to do

Before you choose software or sign up, it helps to know what MTD involves. According to GOV.UK, people within MTD for Income Tax must:

  1. keep digital records of their income and expenses
  2. use compatible software to send quarterly updates of income and expenses to HMRC
  3. sign up before their start date

Signing up does not replace your record-keeping. It tells HMRC you are using MTD. The day-to-day work happens in your records and software.

Step 4: Get your records into digital form

If you already use accounting software, you may be most of the way there. If you use spreadsheets, or paper records, now is the time to decide how you will move to digital records.

Try to have your records in a consistent digital format from the start of the tax year in which your MTD obligations begin. That makes your first quarterly update much easier to prepare. For exactly which records must be kept digitally, and in what detail, check GOV.UK.

Step 5: Choose compatible software

GOV.UK gives three practical pointers.

  • Use the GOV.UK software finder. It lists products that are compatible with MTD for Income Tax.
  • Decide which type of software you need. You can choose software that creates and keeps your digital records, or bridging software that works with the spreadsheets you already use and sends the required information to HMRC.
  • Check it fits your situation. Make sure the software supports all your income sources (for example, self-employment, property, or both) and your accounting period.

GOV.UK also notes that free options exist for people with simple tax affairs, so paid software is not the only route.

When full accounting software may suit you

If you want one place to record income and expenses, track receipts, and prepare your quarterly updates, record-keeping software may suit you better than a spreadsheet plus bridging software. Xero is one option of this kind. Before choosing it, or any other product, confirm through the GOV.UK software finder that it supports your income sources and accounting period.

Step 6: Sign up before your start date

With your income checked, your start date known and your software chosen, you can sign up. You must do this before your start date.

The facts in this guide do not cover the sign-up service itself, such as which login details or information you need, how agents sign clients up, or how long the process takes. Check the sign-up guidance on GOV.UK for the current process. Have your business and property details ready, because you will need to know which income sources you are signing up for.

If an accountant or agent handles your tax, ask them early whether they will sign you up or whether you should do it yourself.

Step 7: Know your quarterly update periods and deadlines

After you are signed up, you send quarterly updates through your software. There are two ways to set the periods, and both have the same deadlines.

Standard period Calendar period Deadline
6 April to 5 July 1 April to 30 June 7 August
6 April to 5 October 1 April to 30 September 7 November
6 April to 5 January 1 April to 31 December 7 February
6 April to 5 April 1 April to 31 March 7 May

To find out how you choose between standard and calendar periods, and whether your software handles this, check GOV.UK and your software provider's guidance.

Quarterly updates are cumulative

Each quarterly update covers the period from the start of the tax year to the end of that update period. If you find a mistake in an earlier quarter, you can correct it in your records and the next update will include the correction. You do not need to resubmit earlier quarters.

Quarterly updates are summaries, not tax returns. For details of what you must send at the end of the tax year to finalise your tax position, check GOV.UK.

Late updates in the first year

HMRC will not give penalty points for late quarterly updates for the 2026 to 2027 tax year. This gives early joiners time to adjust. It applies only to quarterly updates for that tax year, so it is still worth building a routine from the start. For how penalties work in later years, check GOV.UK.

Step 8: Build a routine

MTD works best when records are kept up to date throughout the quarter, rather than gathered just before a deadline. A simple routine might be:

  • record income and expenses weekly or monthly
  • review your figures shortly after each period ends
  • send the update before the deadline in the table above

Good software can help by reminding you of deadlines and showing what has been recorded, but the routine itself is what keeps things manageable.

Quick checklist

  1. Work out your qualifying income (turnover from self-employment and property, using your share of jointly owned property).
  2. Compare it with the thresholds to find your start date.
  3. Understand the three requirements: digital records, compatible software, and signing up before your start date.
  4. Move your records into digital form.
  5. Choose compatible software using the GOV.UK software finder.
  6. Sign up before your start date. Check GOV.UK for the current process.
  7. Note your quarterly periods and deadlines.
  8. Set up a regular record-keeping routine.

If anything about your situation is unusual, such as several businesses, jointly owned property, or partnership income, check the relevant GOV.UK guidance or speak to a qualified adviser before you sign up.
