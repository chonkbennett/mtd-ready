MTD from April 2028: what the £20,000 threshold means

Making Tax Digital (MTD) for Income Tax is being introduced in stages. The third stage, from 6 April 2028, uses a £20,000 threshold. It will bring in many more sole traders and landlords than the earlier stages. This guide explains how the threshold works, what counts towards it, and how to prepare.

The three MTD for Income Tax start dates

According to GOV.UK, MTD for Income Tax is required for sole traders and landlords in three stages:

  • From 6 April 2026, if your qualifying income was more than £50,000 in the 2024 to 2025 tax year.
  • From 6 April 2027, if your qualifying income was more than £30,000 in the 2025 to 2026 tax year.
  • From 6 April 2028, if your qualifying income was more than £20,000 in the 2026 to 2027 tax year.

Each start date depends on your income in an earlier tax year, not the year in which you start. For the April 2028 stage, the year that matters is 2026 to 2027. That tax year starts on 6 April 2026 and ends on 5 April 2027. Your records for that year will decide whether you need to start in 2028.

The threshold is "more than £20,000". Qualifying income of exactly £20,000 is not over the threshold. If your figure is close to it, check the GOV.UK eligibility guidance carefully.

What counts as "qualifying income"

This is the part people most often misunderstand. Qualifying income is not your profit, and it is not your total income from all sources.

GOV.UK defines qualifying income as your total income from self-employment and property before expenses. In other words, it is your turnover. So a sole trader with high costs and modest profits could still be over the threshold.

These do not count towards qualifying income:

  • employment (PAYE) income
  • your share of profit from a partnership
  • dividends
  • the State Pension
  • private pensions

For jointly owned property, only your share of the income counts.

Worked examples from GOV.UK

GOV.UK gives two examples:

  • Someone with £25,000 of rental income and £27,000 of self-employment income has £52,000 of qualifying income. The two sources are added together.
  • A jointly owned property earning £50,000, owned equally, gives each owner £25,000 of qualifying income.

Both of those people would be over the £20,000 threshold. They would also be over the earlier thresholds, so they may need to start before 2028. Always check each stage against the relevant tax year.

Applying it to the £20,000 threshold

Suppose a person earns a salary through PAYE. They also have £12,000 of rental income and £9,000 of turnover from a small sole trader business in the 2026 to 2027 tax year. Their salary does not count. Their qualifying income is £12,000 plus £9,000, which is £21,000. That is more than £20,000, so on these figures they would fall within the April 2028 stage. This is true even if their profit from the business and the property is much lower.

For example, if the same person's rental income had been £10,000 instead, their qualifying income would be £19,000. That would be below the April 2028 threshold.

These are illustrations only. Work out your own figure using the GOV.UK guidance on qualifying income.

What you must do once you are within MTD

GOV.UK says people within MTD for Income Tax must:

  1. Keep digital records of their self-employment and property income and expenses.
  2. Use compatible software to send quarterly updates of income and expenses to HMRC.
  3. Sign up before their start date.

For how and when to sign up, check GOV.UK. An agent can also sign you up with your permission (see below).

Quarterly updates: periods and deadlines

If you use standard periods, your quarterly updates cover:

Update period Deadline
6 April to 5 July 7 August
6 April to 5 October 7 November
6 April to 5 January 7 February
6 April to 5 April 7 May

You can choose calendar periods instead. These end on 30 June, 30 September, 31 December and 31 March. The deadlines are the same.

If you start on 6 April 2028 and use standard periods, your first update would cover 6 April to 5 July 2028. It would be due by 7 August 2028.

Two points from GOV.UK are worth knowing:

  • Updates are cumulative. Each update covers the period from the start of the tax year to the end of that update period. If you spot a mistake in an earlier quarter, you can correct it in a later update. You do not need to resubmit the earlier quarters.
  • Updates are summaries, not tax returns. They report your income and expenses so far. They do not replace the process of finalising your tax for the year. For year-end requirements, check GOV.UK.

Penalties

GOV.UK says HMRC will not give penalty points for late quarterly updates for the 2026 to 2027 tax year. That relief applies to that year only. Do not assume the same will apply when you join in April 2028. Check GOV.UK for the penalty rules that apply to your first year.

Choosing software

GOV.UK recommends these steps:

  • Use the GOV.UK software finder to find compatible products.
  • Decide between two types of software. One type creates digital records for you. The other is bridging software, which works with spreadsheets you already keep.
  • Check that the software supports your income sources (self-employment, property, or both) and your accounting period.
  • Remember that free options exist for people with simple tax affairs.

If you already keep good spreadsheets, bridging software may be enough. If you would prefer to move your bookkeeping into one system, full accounting software is the alternative. Whatever you consider, make sure it appears in the GOV.UK software finder and covers your income types.

Xero is one accounting software option you could compare. Before choosing it, confirm that it is listed in the GOV.UK software finder and that it supports your income sources and accounting period. Compare it with free and bridging options too. For transparency: Xero runs an affiliate programme.

Getting help from an accountant or bookkeeper

Under MTD for Income Tax, you can have:

  • One main agent at a time. A main agent can fully manage your MTD for Income Tax.
  • Any number of supporting agents. A supporting agent has limited access and can only see your business and property income details.

An agent, such as an accountant or bookkeeper, can sign you up with your permission. If you already use an accountant, talk to them early about who will keep the records and who will send the updates.

Separate your business money

Sole traders are not legally required to have a business bank account. However, business.gov.uk notes that keeping personal and business finances separate makes accounting simpler:

  • business transactions are easier to find
  • cash flow is easier to track
  • records for tax are simpler

This becomes more useful when you have to report every quarter. Traditional banks can take longer to set up, but they may offer branches, overdrafts and loans. App-based providers can be set up online quickly, but they may offer limited overdrafts.

A preparation checklist for April 2028

  1. Track your 2026 to 2027 turnover from self-employment and property, before expenses. Count only your share of any jointly owned property.
  2. Leave out income that doesn't count, such as PAYE income, pensions, dividends and partnership profit shares.
  3. Check the earlier thresholds too. If your income was over £50,000 in 2024 to 2025, or over £30,000 in 2025 to 2026, you may need to start in 2026 or 2027 instead.
  4. Start keeping digital records before your start date, so the switch is not rushed.
  5. Choose software using the GOV.UK software finder.
  6. Decide on agents if you want professional help.
  7. Sign up before your start date. Check GOV.UK for how to do this.

The £20,000 threshold is based on turnover, not profit, and it is measured in the 2026 to 2027 tax year. If you work out that figure correctly and set up your records early, joining MTD in April 2028 should be straightforward.
