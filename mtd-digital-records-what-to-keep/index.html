Digital records for MTD: what sole traders need to keep

Making Tax Digital (MTD) for Income Tax changes how many sole traders and landlords keep their records and report to HMRC. Instead of gathering paperwork once a year, you keep records digitally and send HMRC regular summaries using compatible software. This guide explains who needs to do this, when it starts, how your records feed into quarterly updates, and how to set up a system that works.

Who needs to keep digital records, and from when

MTD for Income Tax is being phased in based on your qualifying income:

  • From 6 April 2026, if your qualifying income was more than £50,000 in the 2024 to 2025 tax year.
  • From 6 April 2027, if your qualifying income was more than £30,000 in the 2025 to 2026 tax year.
  • From 6 April 2028, if your qualifying income was more than £20,000 in the 2026 to 2027 tax year.

If you are within MTD for Income Tax, you must:

  1. Keep digital records.
  2. Use compatible software to send quarterly updates of your income and expenses to HMRC.
  3. Sign up before your start date.

You can check whether you are eligible, and whether any exemptions apply to you, on GOV.UK.

Working out your qualifying income

Qualifying income is your total income from self-employment and property before expenses, so it is your turnover, not your profit.

These do not count towards qualifying income:

  • Employment (PAYE) income
  • Your share of profit from a partnership
  • Dividends
  • The State Pension
  • Private pensions

For jointly owned property, only your share counts.

GOV.UK gives two examples:

  • £25,000 of rental income plus £27,000 of self-employment income gives £52,000 of qualifying income. Because this is more than £50,000, the 2026 threshold would apply if these were the figures for the 2024 to 2025 tax year.
  • A jointly owned property earning £50,000, owned equally, gives each owner £25,000 of qualifying income from that property.

The second example matters for landlords. A property bringing in a large total rent may put each owner well below a threshold once the income is split.

What "digital records" means in practice

Under MTD, your income and expense records need to be kept digitally, in software that can either create those records itself or connect to them (for example, from a spreadsheet) and send updates to HMRC.

GOV.UK sets out exactly what information each digital record must contain and how long you need to keep records. Check the current GOV.UK guidance on using MTD for Income Tax for those details, as this guide does not list them.

Whatever the exact requirements, these organisational habits make the job easier:

  • Keep each income source separate. If you have a self-employed business and rental property, record them separately so your software can report each one correctly.
  • Record transactions as you go. Updates are quarterly, so leaving everything until the end of the year means four rushed catch-ups instead of one.
  • Keep supporting documents alongside your records. Invoices, receipts and statements are easier to find when they sit with the digital entry they support.
  • Categorise expenses consistently. Your quarterly updates summarise income and expenses, so consistent categories make those summaries accurate.

How your records feed into quarterly updates

Your digital records form the basis of your quarterly updates. There are two ways to set your update periods.

Standard periods:

Update period Deadline
6 April to 5 July 7 August
6 April to 5 October 7 November
6 April to 5 January 7 February
6 April to 5 April 7 May

Calendar periods run 1 April to 30 June, 30 September, 31 December and 31 March, with the same deadlines: 7 August, 7 November, 7 February and 7 May.

Two points make this less daunting than it might sound:

  • Updates are cumulative. Each update covers the period from the start of the tax year to the end of that update period. If you spot a mistake in an earlier quarter's records, you correct it in your records and it is picked up in your next update. You do not need to resubmit earlier quarters.
  • Updates are summaries, not tax returns. They report totals of your income and expenses. They are not a final calculation of your tax.

Penalties in the first year

HMRC will not give penalty points for late quarterly updates for the 2026 to 2027 tax year. This gives you time to get used to the process, but it is still worth aiming to meet each deadline so that good habits are in place for later years. Check GOV.UK for how penalties work after that year and for your other obligations.

Choosing software for your digital records

GOV.UK recommends that you:

  1. Use its software finder to find compatible products.
  2. Choose between software that creates digital records and bridging software that works with spreadsheets.
  3. Check the software supports your income sources (for example, self-employment, UK property, or both) and your accounting period.

Free options exist for people with simple tax affairs, so paid software is not essential for everyone.

Bridging software can suit you if you already keep good spreadsheets and want to carry on using them. Record-keeping software can suit you if you would rather keep everything in one place and move away from spreadsheets altogether.

If you would prefer full accounting software, Xero is one option to consider. Before choosing it or any other product, confirm through the GOV.UK software finder that it supports MTD for Income Tax for your particular income sources and accounting period, and compare the cost with the free options available.

Separate your business and personal finances

Sole traders are not legally required to have a business bank account. However, keeping business and personal money separate makes accounting simpler:

  • Business transactions are easier to find.
  • Cash flow is easier to track.
  • Your records for tax are simpler to keep.

Under MTD, this matters more, because you will be reviewing and summarising your income and expenses four times a year rather than once.

When choosing an account, traditional banks can take longer to set up but may offer branches, overdrafts and loans. App-based providers can be set up online quickly but may offer limited overdrafts. Choose based on what your business needs.

Getting help from an accountant or bookkeeper

You do not have to manage MTD alone. An agent, such as an accountant or bookkeeper, can sign you up for MTD for Income Tax with your permission.

You can have:

  • One main agent at a time, who can fully manage your MTD for Income Tax.
  • Any number of supporting agents, who have limited access and can only see your business and property income details.

For example, you might have an accountant as your main agent and a bookkeeper as a supporting agent who helps keep your records up to date.

A practical checklist

  • ☐ Work out your qualifying income using turnover from self-employment and property only.
  • ☐ Find your start date from the thresholds above.
  • ☐ Check GOV.UK for exactly what each digital record must contain and how long to keep records.
  • ☐ Choose compatible software using the GOV.UK software finder, checking it supports your income sources and accounting period.
  • ☐ Decide between standard and calendar update periods.
  • ☐ Consider a separate bank account for business income and expenses.
  • ☐ Decide whether you want an agent to help, and in what role.
  • ☐ Sign up before your start date.
  • ☐ Note your quarterly deadlines: 7 August, 7 November, 7 February and 7 May.

Summary

Under MTD for Income Tax, digital records are the foundation for everything else. If your records are kept digitally, separated by income source and updated regularly, your quarterly updates become a matter of reviewing and sending summaries. Start by confirming your qualifying income and start date, check the exact record-keeping requirements on GOV.UK, and choose software that fits how you already work, whether that means a free tool, bridging software for spreadsheets, or full accounting software.
