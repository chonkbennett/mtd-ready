MTD Ready

Sole trader and landlord? Handling both incomes under MTD

If you run a business as a sole trader and also let out property, Making Tax Digital (MTD) for Income Tax affects you differently from someone with only one income source. HMRC adds your self-employment and property income together to decide whether you are in scope. This guide explains how that works, when you may need to start, and how to keep both income streams organised.

Why your two incomes are added together

MTD for Income Tax uses a figure called qualifying income to decide who must join. Qualifying income is your total income from self-employment and property before expenses, which means your turnover rather than your profit.

Because both sources are combined, you can fall within MTD even if neither income on its own would cross the threshold. A modest business and a modest rental portfolio can together take you over the limit.

What counts towards qualifying income

Counts: - Self-employment turnover, before expenses - Property income, before expenses - For jointly owned property, only your share of the income

Does not count: - Employment (PAYE) income - Your share of profit from a partnership - Dividends - The State Pension - Private pensions

So if you also have a salaried job or draw a pension, leave those out. Only your sole trader turnover and your share of rental income go into the calculation.

A worked example from GOV.UK

GOV.UK gives this example: £25,000 of rental income plus £27,000 of self-employment income gives £52,000 of qualifying income. Neither figure is above £50,000 on its own, but together they are.

Jointly owned property

If you own a let property with someone else, you count only your share. GOV.UK's example is a jointly owned property earning £50,000, owned equally. Each owner has £25,000 of qualifying income from it. Each owner would then add any self-employment turnover of their own to that £25,000.

When you will need to start

The start date depends on your qualifying income in an earlier tax year:

MTD required from If qualifying income was more than In the tax year
6 April 2026 £50,000 2024 to 2025
6 April 2027 £30,000 2025 to 2026
6 April 2028 £20,000 2026 to 2027

The test is "more than" each amount. Using the GOV.UK example above, if £52,000 was your combined qualifying income for 2024 to 2025, you would be within MTD from 6 April 2026.

Here is an illustrative example of how combining incomes changes things. Suppose in 2025 to 2026 you had £18,000 of self-employment turnover and £15,000 of rental income. Neither figure is above £30,000, but the total of £33,000 is. On those figures, you would need to use MTD from 6 April 2027.

To check your own position, use the eligibility guidance on GOV.UK.

What MTD asks you to do

Once you are within MTD for Income Tax, you must:

  1. Keep digital records of your income and expenses.
  2. Use compatible software to send quarterly updates of income and expenses to HMRC.
  3. Sign up before your start date.

For details of how to sign up, and of what you need to do at the end of the tax year, check GOV.UK. Those steps are not covered by the facts in this guide.

Quarterly update periods and deadlines

The standard update periods and their deadlines are:

Update period (standard) Deadline
6 April to 5 July 7 August
6 April to 5 October 7 November
6 April to 5 January 7 February
6 April to 5 April 7 May

You can choose calendar periods instead: 1 April to 30 June, 30 September, 31 December and 31 March. The deadlines are the same (7 August, 7 November, 7 February and 7 May).

For someone with both a business and lettings, calendar periods may line up more easily with monthly bank statements. Either option keeps the same deadlines, so pick whichever fits how you already keep your records.

Your updates may need to be sent separately for your business and your property income. Check GOV.UK to confirm how updates work for each income source.

Updates are cumulative, which makes corrections easier

Each quarterly update covers the period from the start of the tax year to the end of that update period. If you find a mistake from an earlier quarter, you can correct it in your records and it will be reflected in your next update. You do not need to resubmit the earlier quarter.

This helps people juggling two income sources, where a rental expense can easily get recorded against the business, or the other way round. Quarterly updates are summaries, not tax returns.

The first year: no penalty points for late updates

HMRC will not give penalty points for late quarterly updates in the 2026 to 2027 tax year. If you start in April 2026, this gives you room to settle into the routine.

It is still sensible to aim for the deadlines from the start. The habit will matter in later years. For how penalties work after 2026 to 2027, check GOV.UK.

Keeping two income streams organised

These practical habits can make MTD more manageable when you have both a business and lettings. They are suggestions, not legal requirements:

  • Keep business and property transactions clearly separated. Using different bank accounts, or at least clear labels, makes it easier to send accurate figures for each source.
  • Record your share only for joint property. Make sure your records show the portion that applies to you.
  • Note turnover, not just profit. Qualifying income is measured before expenses, so keep a clear running total of gross income from both sources. This helps you see whether you are near a threshold for a future year.
  • Set a regular routine. Updating records monthly, rather than just before each 7th-of-the-month deadline, spreads the work out.
  • Review before each update. Because updates are cumulative, a quick check each quarter lets you catch mistakes early.

Choosing software that handles both incomes

GOV.UK suggests three steps when choosing software:

  • Use HMRC's software finder on GOV.UK to see compatible products.
  • Choose between two types: software that creates digital records itself, or bridging software that works with spreadsheets you already keep.
  • Check the software supports your income sources and accounting period. For a sole trader and landlord, make sure the product handles both self-employment and property income, and the update periods (standard or calendar) you plan to use.

GOV.UK also notes that free options exist for simple tax affairs. If your business and lettings are straightforward, look at those before paying for anything.

If you prefer full accounting software that keeps your records digitally, Xero is one option to consider. Before you sign up, confirm on HMRC's software finder that it supports both of your income sources and your chosen accounting period. Note that Xero runs a free-to-join affiliate programme, so recommendations of its products, including this one, may be commercially motivated. Judge it against your own needs and compare it with the alternatives, including free and bridging options.

Quick checklist

  • ☐ Add up your self-employment turnover and property income (your share only) for the relevant tax year
  • ☐ Leave out PAYE wages, partnership profit shares, dividends and pensions
  • ☐ Compare the total against the threshold for that year (£50,000, £30,000 or £20,000)
  • ☐ Note your MTD start date if you are over the threshold
  • ☐ Choose standard or calendar update periods
  • ☐ Pick software using HMRC's software finder, checking it supports both income sources
  • ☐ Move to digital records before your start date
  • ☐ Sign up before your start date (see GOV.UK for how)
  • ☐ Put the 7 August, 7 November, 7 February and 7 May deadlines in your calendar

Points to check on GOV.UK

This guide covers the core rules, but some details need confirming directly with HMRC:

  • How to sign up and when to do it
  • Whether separate updates are needed for business and property income
  • What you must do after the end of the tax year
  • How late-submission penalties work after 2026 to 2027
  • Any exemptions that might apply to you

If you have checked your figures and know you will be within MTD, setting up digital records early gives you time to sort out how your business and property transactions are split before your first update is due.
