Do pensions or dividends count towards the MTD threshold?

The short answer

No. Pensions and dividends do not count towards the Making Tax Digital (MTD) for Income Tax threshold.

GOV.UK says the threshold is based on qualifying income, which only includes income from self-employment and property. The following do not count:

  • the State Pension
  • private pensions
  • dividends
  • employment (PAYE) income
  • your share of partnership profit

If you are a retired landlord, a company director taking dividends, or someone with a pension alongside a small business, you only need to look at your self-employment and property income when working out whether MTD applies to you.

What counts as qualifying income

Qualifying income is your total income from self-employment and property before expenses, which is your turnover. This affects the calculation in two ways.

It is turnover, not profit. Expenses are not deducted first. A landlord with high mortgage interest or repair costs, or a sole trader with high running costs, still uses the gross figure.

Self-employment and property income are added together. If you have both, you combine them.

GOV.UK gives this example: £25,000 of rental income plus £27,000 of self-employment income gives £52,000 of qualifying income. Neither figure is above £50,000 on its own, but together they are.

Jointly owned property

If you own property with someone else, only your share of the income counts. In GOV.UK's example, a jointly owned property earning £50,000 and owned equally gives each owner £25,000 of qualifying income.

This matters for couples and family members who let property together. Each person looks at their own share, plus any self-employment or other property income they have in their own name.

What doesn't count

These types of income are excluded from qualifying income:

Income type Counts towards MTD threshold?
Self-employment turnover Yes
Property income (your share) Yes
State Pension No
Private pensions No
Dividends No
Employment (PAYE) income No
Share of partnership profit No

Excluding an income type from the MTD threshold does not mean it disappears from your tax affairs. This article only covers whether it counts towards the threshold. If you are unsure how to report pensions, dividends or other income, check GOV.UK. If an income type is not listed above, such as savings interest, check GOV.UK's qualifying income guidance rather than assuming either way.

Worked examples

GOV.UK's example with a pension added. Take the GOV.UK case of £25,000 rental income plus £27,000 self-employment income, which gives £52,000 of qualifying income. Suppose that person also receives the State Pension and some dividends. Their qualifying income is still £52,000, because neither the pension nor the dividends are added.

A retired landlord. Suppose a retired landlord receives £18,000 a year in rent, plus a private pension and the State Pension. Their qualifying income is £18,000, which is the rent only. The pension income makes no difference to the threshold test. They would compare £18,000 against the threshold that applies to the relevant tax year (see below).

A director with a side business. Suppose a company director takes a salary through PAYE and dividends from their company, and also earns £22,000 of self-employment turnover from freelance work. Only the £22,000 counts as qualifying income. The salary and dividends are both excluded.

These examples only illustrate how the calculation works. They are not predictions about anyone's position.

The thresholds and start dates

MTD for Income Tax is being introduced in stages. Each stage looks back at your qualifying income for an earlier tax year:

  • From 6 April 2026: required if your qualifying income was more than £50,000 in the 2024 to 2025 tax year.
  • From 6 April 2027: required if your qualifying income was more than £30,000 in the 2025 to 2026 tax year.
  • From 6 April 2028: required if your qualifying income was more than £20,000 in the 2026 to 2027 tax year.

The test is "more than", so you need to be above each figure, not equal to it. For each stage, use the tax year listed next to that date.

If you're in scope: what MTD requires

If your qualifying income puts you in scope, GOV.UK says you must:

  1. Sign up for MTD for Income Tax before your start date.
  2. Keep digital records of your self-employment and property income and expenses.
  3. Use compatible software to send quarterly updates of income and expenses to HMRC.

Quarterly update periods and deadlines

Under standard periods, the quarters run from 6 April to 5 July, 5 October, 5 January and 5 April. The deadlines are:

  • 7 August
  • 7 November
  • 7 February
  • 7 May

You can choose calendar periods instead, running from 1 April to 30 June, 30 September, 31 December and 31 March. The deadlines are the same.

Updates are cumulative

Each quarterly update covers the period from the start of the tax year to the end of that quarter. If you spot a mistake in an earlier quarter, you can correct your records and the next update will include the correction. You do not need to resubmit earlier quarters. Quarterly updates are summaries, not tax returns.

HMRC has also said it will not give penalty points for late quarterly updates for the 2026 to 2027 tax year. This applies to that year's quarterly updates only, so check GOV.UK for the position in later years.

Choosing software

GOV.UK recommends three steps:

  • Use the GOV.UK software finder to find compatible products.
  • Decide between software that creates digital records and bridging software that works with spreadsheets you already keep.
  • Check that the software supports your income sources and accounting period. For example, check it handles both self-employment and property income if you have both.

GOV.UK notes that free options exist for people with simple tax affairs. Look at those before paying for anything.

If you would rather keep records in dedicated software than in a spreadsheet, Xero is one option to consider. Before choosing it or any other product, check it appears in the GOV.UK software finder and supports your particular income sources.

Keep business money separate

Sole traders are not legally required to have a business bank account. However, GOV.UK's business guidance says separating personal and business finances makes accounting simpler. Business transactions are easier to find, cash flow is easier to track, and your tax records are simpler.

This is particularly useful if pension payments or dividends land in the same account as rent or business income. Keeping them apart makes it easier to see which income is qualifying income.

When choosing a provider:

  • Traditional banks can take longer to set up but may offer branches, overdrafts and loans.
  • App-based providers set up online quickly but may offer limited overdrafts.

Getting help from an agent

You don't have to manage MTD alone. Under MTD for Income Tax:

  • You can have one main agent at a time. A main agent can fully manage your MTD for Income Tax.
  • You can have any number of supporting agents. These have limited access and can only see business and property income details.
  • An agent, such as an accountant or bookkeeper, can sign you up with your permission.

An agent can be helpful if you have a mix of pension, dividend, property and self-employment income and want someone to confirm your qualifying income figure.

Quick checklist

  • ☐ Add up your self-employment turnover and property income (your share only) for the relevant tax year.
  • ☐ Leave out pensions, dividends, PAYE income and partnership profit shares.
  • ☐ Compare the total with the threshold for that stage (more than £50,000, £30,000 or £20,000).
  • ☐ If you're in scope, sign up before your start date.
  • ☐ Choose compatible software using the GOV.UK software finder.
  • ☐ Consider a separate account for business or rental income.

If you have decided on software and want a tool that keeps digital records for quarterly updates, Xero is one to compare against the free and bridging options on GOV.UK.
